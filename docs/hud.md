# The HUD on screen

## Overview

Each display has its own HUD and settings. Notch mode uses the physical notch or a stand-in bar; Dynamic mode places agent logos along any screen edge; Simple mode parks a small panel of rings and numbers on an edge. The glow forms a rim around the island or a backdrop behind the logos, and Simple mode has none. Each display's HUD also has a look: dark, light or matching macOS, solid or glass; the island in the row of a hardware notch stays dark.

## Model

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HUD | One display's presentation: a collapsed entry on a screen edge, the panel it opens into, and its glow |
| Notch mode | The island: the physical notch on a Mac that has one, or a bar standing in for it on a display that does not |
| Dynamic mode | A queue of marks — the watched vendors and any run in the last day — along a screen edge with no shape behind them |
| Simple mode | A small floating panel on a screen edge with no glow behind it: each agent's mark, with its most used window as a ring round the mark, a percentage beside it, or both |
| Glow | Colour drawn from the enabled windows' levels — a rim around the island, a backdrop falling inward behind the dock |
| Bot | What a mark turns into to act out what its agents are doing: a small body in the mark's own outline and colour, with two eyes; Claude's is Clawd |
| Placement | What one display shows and where: mode, edge, position along the edge, logo size, logo spacing, whether the marks are drawn, and the Simple panel's size, ring stroke, rings and numbers, with the HUD's look |
| Look | How one display's HUD is drawn: its appearance, Dark, Light or Match system, its material, Solid or glass in one of three styles, Clear, Regular or Material, and that material's Transparency, for the HUD at rest, its panel and its alerts and cards alike |
| Panel layout | How a screen's hover panel differs from its default view — what is hidden, in what order, which widget draws each block, which measures its picker offers and whether it takes the whole line or half of one, and how wide and how spaced the whole panel is — and which agents that screen's resting HUD shows. A Mac has a shared layout and, for any screen, one of its own |
| Style draft | An agent's unsaved change to one screen's resting HUD — its mode, edge, sizes, look and glow — drawn over that screen's placement and glow until a person saves it into them or drops it |
| App draft | An agent's unsaved change to the app's own settings — its Appearance, Match screen, Bot logos, menu-bar icon and Hover behavior — made only when the person asks for that setting and drawn over them for every screen until a person saves it into them or drops it |

## Rules

### Placement

- A display with a notch defaults to notch mode; a display without one defaults to Dynamic mode, so no screen draws a bar pretending to have a notch.
- Any mode can be chosen for any display; notch mode stays at the physical notch or the screen's top centre. Choosing a docked mode in settings for a display with a notch moves a HUD still at top centre to the top left, clear of the hardware.
- A display keeps its own placement under its UUID; display settings can reset its position to top centre, or top left for a dock on a notched display, keeping its mode and logo settings.
- The dock can sit anywhere along the top, right, bottom or left edge, running horizontally on the top and bottom and vertically on the sides; logo size is 20–60 pt, spacing is 0.1–0.6 of the logo, and the logos stand 0–20 pt in from the edge.
- Drag a visible HUD directly to any edge; dragging a notch switches it to Dynamic mode, and a Simple panel stays Simple. Its bounds appear while it is grabbed or dragged.
- The marks can be hidden, which leaves the backdrop alone, still where they would have been and as wide.
- An agent can propose a change to one display's placement and glow through the MCP tools; it is drawn at once as that display's [style draft](#style-drafts), and only a person's Save writes it into these settings.

### Look

- Each display's HUD has a look: an appearance, Dark, the HUD as it has always been and the default, Light, or Match system, which follows macOS's own light or dark setting and never the app's Appearance in Settings → General; a material, Solid, the default, or glass in one of three styles, Clear, Regular or Material, Clear until another is chosen; and a Transparency for Solid and one for glass. One look covers everything the display's HUD draws: the island at rest, the Dynamic dock and the Simple panel, the hover panel, and the alerts, requests and cards.
- Glass is the system's own glass: the desktop shows through it, blurred, and it is light or dark as the look's appearance and the desktop behind it make it, lighter over a white window and darker over a black desktop. Clear is macOS 26's clear Liquid Glass, the same at any size, which shows the most of the desktop. Regular is its regular Liquid Glass, with more of its own frost, which turns lighter or darker with the size of the piece it is drawn in. The HUD's pieces sit side by side, the dock, its hover line and a card, and in regular glass they parted in colour over the same window, a narrow strip white beside a dark dock, so the HUD draws Regular as clear Liquid Glass too, the same as Clear, and only a piece on its own draws the regular glass: a window that takes the look with Match screen on, and the menu-bar menu. The owner chose this. Material is SwiftUI's regular material, an even frosted blur without Liquid Glass's lit rim, drawn the same on every macOS, so it keeps the hairline a solid look draws round it. Clear and Regular are a frosted blur on macOS 14 and 15 and in a build made with an Xcode older than 26. With Reduce Transparency on, every glass is drawn Solid, and every surface opaque whatever its Transparency.
- Each material has a Transparency of its own, set on a slider of 0–100 % for both and saved as it is set, and the two mean the same: how much of the look's own colour, black on Dark and near-white on Light, is taken away. Solid, Clear and Regular draw three fifths of it, so that their 100 % draws 60 %, and Material draws all of it. The owner chose this: a solid fill or clear glass any thinner leaves the HUD's ink on all but the bare desktop, and regular Liquid Glass with little of the look's colour beneath it turns light or dark by itself, piece by piece by the size of each, so that a narrow strip came out white beside a dark dock; Material frosts what is behind it evenly on its own. So on Solid, Clear and Regular all of the colour is left at 0 %, 70 % at 50 % and 40 % at 100 %, and on Material all of it at 0 %, half at 50 % and none at 100 %, the bare material. Glass's is 50 % by default and Solid's 0 %, opaque. Solid's thins the fill, so the desktop shows through it unblurred; at 0 % every surface is opaque, the Simple panel at rest and the line that grows out of it too, which were drawn slightly see-through before the HUD had a transparency. Glass's sets how much of the colour lies beneath the glass: all of it at 0 %, where the glass shows that colour alone, blurred, 40 % at the top on Clear and Regular, and none on Material, the system's bare material. Over a material and over the frosted blur, which hide whatever lies beneath them in the window, the colour lies over the glass instead, as strong. The glass itself is always drawn whole and blurred, never faded: Apple's documentation of clear Liquid Glass asks for a dimming layer beneath it to keep what is on it legible, a colour laid over Liquid Glass hid the glass, and Liquid Glass given an opacity loses its blur altogether. The HUD's ink is fixed by the appearance, light on Dark and dark on Light, so the less colour is left, under either material, the less well it reads over a desktop of the other shade: Dark's light text over a white window, Light's dark text over a black desktop. Where between is the user's choice. The glass lies behind what the HUD holds, never round it and never in a glass container with it, so text and marks stay sharp and keep their colours.
- What the HUD paints on its ground thins with the ground wherever the desktop shows through, glass at any Transparency or Solid above 0 %, so that it follows the look on Light as it does on Dark: a card, a row's box and a text field, opaque on Light, keep a fifth plus four fifths of the colour the Transparency leaves as it is drawn, 76 % at 50 % and 52 % at 100 % on Solid, Clear and Regular, and a fifth at Material's 100 %, never below a fifth, as the Dark look's cards, tints of white, already let the ground through; on Dark the text fields thin the same way. The greys there, secondary and tertiary text, an ended session's dot, and the ring and figure of an agent with no level to show on the Simple panel, are tints of the ink, white on Dark and black on Light, each at the share that gives the very grey on the look's own colour, so that nothing moves as Solid's Transparency leaves 0 %: a fixed grey lies inside the range of grounds glass can reach, mid-grey for Dark over a light desktop and for Light over a dark one, and ran into the ground and into its own track there, where a tint always lies between the ground and the ink. On light glass secondary and tertiary text are a grade darker, at any Transparency, since glass over a dark desktop is a greyer ground than the solid surface. A level as text or a thin glyph, such as a quota's figure, and an alert's colour as text take paler shades on Dark and deeper ones on Light there; a level's shades hold about 1.5 to 2.2:1 on a Dark ground at its lightest and 3 to 3.9:1 on a Light one at half its Transparency over black, where the opaque look's shades fell to 1.2 to 1.6 and 2.2 to 2.4. Their fills, a level's rings, bars and dots and an alert's main button, keep their hues, and the main ink and the hairlines keep their colours. On Light, solid or glass, a request's Deny is filled and outlined, and Always allow and a plan's Approve, accept edits outlined, in the light HUD's own level fills rather than the bright hues the Dark HUD's are: the outlines in its deeper green, where the bright green at a fraction all but vanishes on near-white and a button reads as loose text, and Deny in its red, the system's light red, all but the Dark HUD's. The ring round a session row's status dot is cut out of the agent's mark, so the ground shows in it rather than a ring of the window's colour.
- The details a token bar shows on hover take the panel's whole look, drawn nearly opaque, so that the bars, the grid and the labels beneath them never show through their text: on glass they stand on glass's stand-in, the look's own colour at 85 % plus 15 % of what lies beneath the glass, all of it at 0 %, about 96 % at 50 % and 91 % at 100 % on Clear and Regular, and 85 % at Material's 100 %, so they keep a ground and stand off the panel under them, since glass shows nothing through glass; on Solid their fill thins as the panel's does, but never below nine tenths. The details a quota row shows on hover, its forecast and its reset credits, open in a window of their own on a surface of the look, real glass on glass, which keeps its blur, and on Solid a fill never below nine tenths too. Both cast no shadow on a see-through look, where a shadow would darken what shows through them, and keep it on an opaque one.
- Notch mode on a display with a hardware notch keeps the notch's row dark whatever the display's appearance, so the row stays one with the camera housing: the island at rest is black, as the standard island is, hairline included, and a reminder's wings are black, opaque under Solid at any Transparency, or black glass under glass: the display's glass style with 80 % black beneath it, or over it on a material or the frosted blur, whatever the Transparency, since bare glass would turn light over a light menu bar. The panel and the cards it opens into, a request's card among them, take the display's whole look, and opening and closing cross-fade between the two on the island's curve. In any look but the standard one the island draws the collapsed island's own shape, the housing and its flares, in black over its surface, under its hairline, and fades that black out over 14 pt round the housing, so the housing melts into a light or glass panel instead of meeting it at a line; the black fades in and out with the look. On a light look what the panel and a card hold starts those 14 pt lower, clear of the fade. A notch-style bar on a display without a notch takes the whole look, as every dock does.
- On a Simple panel that separates its agents, under Clear or Regular drawn as Liquid Glass, the panel's glass and that of the hover line that grows out of it are one piece of Liquid Glass: drawn in one `GlassEffectContainer`, they flow into each other with a smooth fillet that follows the line as it grows and shrinks, where two pieces side by side each lit a rim and the panel's lit a bright band down the line's end. The container holds the two pieces of glass and nothing else, never the figures, the marks or the line's text, which it would blur as if they were the desktop. The look's colour lies beneath both, outside the container, the line's kept out from under the panel's, so that the two never darken each other. Only the line joins the panel: a full card, 6 pt off it, and an event's card, a reminder or a request, keep a surface of their own, and under Solid, Material, the frosted blur, or glass's stand-in in a picture or a recording, the panel and the line each keep their own, as they always have. The line hands over to the full card or an event's card at once, while the card's frame goes on growing, since two half-strength dimmings cross-fading would lighten the card meanwhile and Liquid Glass faded by opacity loses its blur; a change of look that brings or takes the joined glass fades the line and the panel together.
- A light look, and one the desktop shows through, gives each of the HUD's surfaces a shadow of its own, kept out from under it where the desktop shows through it; the standard look keeps the one shadow it has always had, under the island and only with the glow. On a dock that separates its agents, a card's shadow, drawn where the card ends up, with the card cut out of it on a see-through look, waits until the card has grown out of its dock or slid to its place, 0.4 s after its last change, and then fades in over 0.4 s, while on a see-through look the dock's shadow, cut by the card from then on, fades to its new cut with it; shown at once, its cut-out showed on a light desktop as a pale pill with a shadow round it, ahead of the growing line. A card whose shadow shows keeps it as it slides to another mark, and the shadow follows it, while the dock's cut fades to the card's new place as the card goes there. Under Reduce Motion, where the card is placed at once, so is its shadow. A card going back into its dock drops its shadow at once, which fading there would show the cut-out place round the shrinking card; every other surface's shadow that goes, such as the island's grown over the panel for an event from an agent with no mark, fades out where it was.
- A change of look cross-fades on the island's curve, 0.4 s, whatever changes it: Settings, an agent's style draft, Save, Discard or a reset, macOS turning light or dark under Match system, or Reduce Transparency being switched. Reduce Motion applies it at once.
- Settings → Display → Screens sets the look of the display chosen there in rows under its mode: HUD appearance, Dark, Light or Match system; Material, one choice of Solid, Clear, Regular or Material (实色, Clear, Regular, 磨砂 in Chinese), which keeps the glass style while Solid is chosen; and the Transparency slider of the material chosen, 0–100 % in steps of 5 % for either, each keeping its own, of which Solid, Clear and Regular draw three fifths and Material all. On a display with a notch in Notch mode the appearance row says that the notch's row stays dark; with Reduce Transparency on and glass chosen the Material row says that Clear, Regular and Material show as solid, and with Solid above 0 % it says that the Transparency is not drawn. The HUD style row's Reset to Default gives Dark and Solid back, with Clear glass at 50 % and Solid at 0 %. The previews in Settings draw Clear and Regular as Liquid Glass over the wallpaper painted in them; Material, and the frosted blur before macOS 26 and in a build made with an Xcode older than 26, blur only the desktop behind the window, never what is painted in it, so for those the previews draw glass's stand-in.
- `SettingsStore.effectiveLook(on:)` resolves a display's look in one place, for its HUD, the pictures an agent asks for and the Settings previews: from the placement the display draws now, a style draft included, macOS's light or dark setting and Reduce Transparency. `restingLook(on:)` is the look of the HUD at rest, the standard one at a hardware notch, which the glow's preview and the picture of the resting HUD draw. A HUD being dragged keeps the look its display has saved until it is dropped.
- The pictures an agent asks for are drawn off screen, with no desktop behind them, so glass in them is a stand-in, the same for Clear, Regular and Material but for the share of the Transparency each draws, and the tools say so: the look's own colour, black or near-white, at 35 % plus 65 % of the colour that lies beneath the glass, so all of it at 0 %, about four fifths at 50 % and 61 % at 100 % on Clear and Regular, and 35 % at Material's 100 %, which still holds the HUD's ink in a picture of the bare material. The notch row's black glass stands in at 87 % black at any Transparency. A recording of the HUD draws glass the same way.
- Settings → General has a Match screen switch below Appearance, tagged Experimental, which greys the Appearance out while on: each of the app's windows, statistics, Settings and onboarding, and the menu-bar menu then take the look of the HUD on the screen they are on, as its panel draws it, its appearance, material, glass style and Transparency, and a window taken to another screen takes that one's, at once. Whenever the desktop shows through that look, glass at any Transparency or Solid above 0 %, the window is cleared, and its shadow is made again from what it then draws. On a screen of glass the ground of its page and the Settings sidebar are the screen's glass, Clear, Regular or Material, drawn whole, Regular as the regular Liquid Glass here, since a window is one piece, with the window's own colour beneath Liquid Glass, or over a material or the frosted blur: all of it at 0 %, where the glass shows the window's colour alone, 70 % at 50 %, glass's default, and 40 % at 100 % on Clear and Regular, and half at 50 % and none at 100 % on Material, the Transparency drawn as the HUD draws it. On a Solid screen the ground is the window's colour at what the Transparency leaves of it, 70 % at 50 % and 40 % at 100 %, with the desktop showing through it unblurred; at 0 % the window stays opaque. The cards, rows and text fields on the ground thin with it as the HUD's do, on a light window as on a dark one, Settings' groups and a host's pages that stand on them included: a light window's white cards keep 76 % at 50 % and 52 % at 100 % on Solid, Clear and Regular, and a fifth at Material's 100 %, never below a fifth. Its greys, the Settings sidebar's names on Dark among them, are tints of the ink as the HUD's are, at the shares that give them on the window's own colour, secondary and tertiary text a grade darker on light glass, and a level's and an alert's colour as text and a chart's hover details take the shades and the floors they take in the HUD. A link, such as Reveal log in Finder or Back to default selection, and Copy command in Settings → Experimental are the main ink, underlined, where the system's blue falls to about 1:1 on a mid-grey ground. The faint tints that mark a state, a hovered session, the agent chosen in Settings → Agents, a model chosen in the token chart's legend and the Settings sidebar's edge, are about twice as strong, since a tint's step shrinks as the ground nears the ink. The heatmap's busy hours are the theme's green over the ground, all of it at the busiest hour and thinner as an hour is quieter, so that the contrast rises with the hour, where the green ramp's middle could be as bright as the ground. The main ink, the other charts and the other hairlines keep their colours. On every ground, opaque too, an empty hour of the heatmap is a tint of the ink, white at 6 % on Dark and black at 7.5 % on Light, and a chosen segment on Dark white at 24 % over its track, so that each stays a step off what lies under it whatever the ground; a chosen segment is never thinned, and on Light it is white, as macOS draws a chosen control. The ring round the last point of a session's context chart is cut out, so the ground shows in it. One ground lies under each part of a window, never glass over glass, which would not see the desktop: the Settings sidebar's border is drawn on the sidebar's own ground, so no slit of desktop shows beside it, and onboarding's ground fills the window. macOS draws clear Liquid Glass in the window in use all but unblurred, so a window of Clear shows the desktop behind it almost sharp while it is the active one, and frosted once it is in the background; this is how the system draws it, and the switch's note says so. Before macOS 26 and in a build made with an Xcode older than 26, Clear and Regular are a frosted blur here too. It is saved as `appearanceFollowsScreen` beside the Appearance, which a build without the switch reads as it is. An agent the person asks can propose either as the [app draft](#app-drafts), which the windows and the menu take at once. The HUDs never follow the app's Appearance.
- The menu-bar menu is a panel Agent HUD draws itself, with the lines it has always had, in the same order unless a layout lists the agents its screen's HUD shows ([panel layout](#panel-layout)), so that it can take a look as the HUD does, where a system menu can only be light or dark. With Match screen on it takes the look of the HUD on the screen whose menu bar it opens from, as a window there does; with it off, the app's Appearance, light or dark, on regular Liquid Glass at glass's default Transparency, 50 %, drawn at three fifths as a display's is, so that 70 % of its colour lies beneath it, which reads as a macOS menu does, a frosted blur before macOS 26 and in a build made with an Xcode older than 26, and solid and opaque under Reduce Transparency. It is one piece, as a window is, so it draws Regular as the regular glass, with Match screen on or off, where the HUD's pieces draw it clear (`drawsRegularGlass`). It takes a change of look while it is open, as the HUD does. Its rows take the look's ink: an agent's name 13 pt bold, a row 13 pt, an account's header 11 pt in grey, and a quota's dot and a critical figure in the HUD's status colours for the look, a shade deeper on Light, the figure on a see-through look in the paler or deeper shade the HUD's text takes there; the row under the pointer or the arrow keys is drawn in the system's selection colour with white text, as a menu's is. It has the look's hairline wherever Liquid Glass lights no rim of its own, 10 pt corners, and no shadow on a see-through look. It opens as the status item is pressed, with either button, its left edge under the item and 4 pt below the menu bar, kept 4 pt inside the screen, at least 250 pt wide and as wide as its widest row, and the item stays highlighted while it is open. It takes the keys without bringing Agent HUD to the front, so the app in front stays in front, and macOS draws Clear glass sharper in it for that, as in the window in use. Up and Down move the highlight over the rows that can be chosen, past headers, separators and rows that are off, round from the last to the first, and with none highlighted Down takes the first and Up the last; Return, Enter and Space choose the highlighted row and Escape closes the menu; while it is open ⌘, opens Settings, ⌘Q quits, ⌥⌘H shows or hides the glow and a host item's shortcut chooses that item, and any other key does nothing, without a beep. A click chooses a row, and the menu closes before the row acts, so a window it opens comes up over no menu. A second click of the status item closes it, and so do a click in another app or in another of Agent HUD's windows, the island included, Agent HUD ceasing to be the active app, a change of displays or of Space, and the menu-bar icon being hidden. On Escape, on a chosen row, on a second click of the status item, on a click in another of Agent HUD's windows and on a change of displays or Space, the window that had the keys before gets them back, a HUD card's field only while it is still there; after a click in another app, or when Agent HUD stops being active or the menu loses the keys to another window, they stay where the user sent them. VoiceOver reads it as a menu of menu items, each its title and then its figure, with its tooltip as help, and a press chooses one. Unlike a system menu it does not choose the row a press on the status item is dragged to and let go over, it has no submenus and no choosing by a typed letter, and it does not scroll: on a screen too short for it, it keeps its top under the menu bar and leaves out the rows at the bottom that do not fit, so that Settings…, the glow switch, Quit and a host item can then be reached only by their shortcuts, ⌘, ⌥⌘H and ⌘Q, and a host item without one not at all.

### What the queue shows

- The enabled, non-API-billed agents first, in the order they are watched in, then any vendor that ran in the last day without a window on that list, most recently used first.
- Watched vendors appear before their first reading; vendors shown only for recent activity leave a day after their last turn and never appear with Live status off.
- Shared artwork appears once: Claude windows share one mark, as do Codex and ChatGPT.
- A mark acts out what its agents are doing ([bots](#bots)): its bot while a session works or waits on the user, using the same liveness as the panel, and its logo while they rest, with a visit as its bot now and then. With `botLogos` off, Reduce Motion on, or for a mark that has no bot, it stays its logo and keeps still, wearing a waiting request's or a badge's sign.
- A screen whose layout lists its agents ([panel layout](#panel-layout)) shows those of them it would show anyway, in the list's order, and no other mark.
- A dock with nothing to show keeps a minimal entry at its saved position without drawing a logo.
- Marks retain their artwork with a hairline outline; single-colour marks are white, and status colour appears in the glow.

### Bots

- Every bundled mark has a bot. Its body is a plain ball or the mark's own simple shape, in the mark's colours, with any feature drawn on the body: Pi a square on two legs under a flat hat, Grok a ball with a ring round its waist, Hermes a ball with a fringe, Qoder a ball in a green neck warmer, CodeBuddy and WorkBuddy an upright cat. DeepSeek is a fat fish: a ball with a pale belly, a fin on its back and a small triangular fin low on each side for hands, all three behind the body; the hands flap on a tool's beats, at a start and over a reply, and the one on the pointer's side waves at a greeting. GitHub Copilot keeps its artwork and only its eyes move.
- Claude's bot is Clawd, Claude Code's own creature. Clawd plays the Claude app's own sprites, from Clawd Quest, the game the Claude desktop app ships. The 18 its clips use are bundled with Agent HUD, copied unchanged from Claude 2.37337.1 into `Sources/AgentHUDDesktop/Resources/clawd-sprites.json` and credited in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`, and decoded once, the first time a Claude mark is built; Agent HUD never reads the Claude app itself. `scripts/clawd-sprites.py` copies them again from an installed Claude app, by hand, and writes nothing when one of them has been redrawn with another number of frames than the clips were written for. A debug build's `--snapshot` draws them through every clip as `clawd-sprites-*`, for checking the clips by eye. The demo and a recording play them too. On a mark too small for the sprites, Clawd is drawn by Agent HUD itself as pixel art in the logo's orange, moving in whole steps of its art.
- Sprites are drawn in whole device pixels and never scaled, turned or morphed: a sprite pixel is n/2 device pixels, n = max(1, ⌊1.8 · side · display scale / 24 + 0.05⌋), and the sprites are used from n = 2, a 26 pt mark on a standard display and a 13 pt one on Retina. Each frame is baked once, with its sign and hairline, at most 64 kept for each mark and let go once it has rested as its logo for a minute, the visits of a resting mark counted in. The logo and the sprites fade into each other over 0.3 s.
- A mark shows one look at a time, may play one event over it, and may wear one badge. One director decides them for every screen, from what each session's log and the clients' structured hooks say ([session lifecycle](session-lifecycle.md#what-a-turn-is-doing)); never from wording, and a quiet log is never a finished or failed turn.
- Looks, the first that applies:
  - A request waiting on the user: an approval before a question before a plan, and of two alike the one that has waited longest.
  - Claude compacting a conversation on its own, from the moment its compaction hook runs until its log moves again, five minutes at most.
  - Work, as one session does it: thinking, a command, an edit, a read, a shell command open for 20 s or more, or plain work for a client whose log names no tools. The session shown keeps the mark for as long as it works; when it stops, the session whose log wrote last takes over, and the mark never mixes two sessions' looks. A tool's look outlasts its call by 10 s, so a quick call between thoughts does not flash past, and then thinking shows. Thinking holds still after three quiet minutes, or while a failed request is tried again. Sub-agents at work stand beside the body as a small still copy of it, except while Clawd sits at its desk for a long command, which leaves no room for one.
  - No news: a session in flight by evidence over 30 minutes old, and none working. The logo at half strength, with no visits.
  - Idle: the logo, with a visit as its bot of three to five seconds every 20 to 35 seconds.
  - Asleep, once every session of the mark has been idle for an hour, or it has none: the logo, with a doze every three to five minutes.
- A new look of the same work shows once two reads at least 2 s apart want it, and not before the one shown has held for 6 s; what is wanted meanwhile waits, the newest winning. After four changes within 30 s a look holds 12 s, until 30 s pass with no change. Work shows at once when it begins or another session takes over, a request or a compaction cuts in at once, and so does the work after a compaction, and a turn's end goes to rest at once.
- Events: a start, when work begins after five minutes of rest or from sleep, on a prompt given during that rest and since Agent HUD started, with nothing waiting, so a turn woken without a prompt starts nothing; a failure or a limit, when a turn ends on one of the structured ends a client writes, also since Agent HUD started. One waits at a time, a newer one taking its place unless the one waiting outranks it (failure, then limit, then start), and one that cannot begin within 3 s is let go. A failure or a limit that comes while a card hides the dock plays once when the dock comes back, if its badge still stands; a start missed so is over. A reply is the card's alone: the dock never plays it.
- Badges, still and with no clock: `×` after a failed turn, until a prompt in any of the agent's sessions, until its card or the whole panel is opened, or for 15 minutes; `⧗` while a turn's limit holds, until the reset the client gave, else until one of the vendor's quota windows begins anew or a prompt in any of the agent's sessions, or while one of the vendor's quota windows is used up, which never takes the place of work.
- A sign stands over the bot's head, a 5×7 grid in whole device pixels, two pixels a cell at 20 pt on Retina: `!` for an approval, `?` a question, `≡` a plan, `×` and `⧗` the badges, `…` a thinking bot's dots, `z` a doze, one at a time in that order. Its ink is white inside a dark edge on a dark ground and dark on a light one, never the body's colour. Clawd says it thinks with its own clip, and shows a plan with its bulb.
- How each look moves. Other bots: thinking looks up and to one side, then the other every 2.5 s, its head tilted 5° toward the gaze; a tool bobs on the beat in bursts of 3 s with rests of 1.5 s, and a spinning body spins in the bursts; plain work is the working motion bots have always had. A request hops, rises by 12% and makes a move 1.2 s in and then every 2.6 s, every 8 s once it has waited two minutes: a knock for an approval, a tilt of 12° to the other side for a question, two nods for a plan. A start hops while the eyes open; a failure shakes and sags, with crossed eyes on the nine bots whose eyes are big enough and a squint on the rest; a limit sighs. Clawd plays its clips: thinking, the laptop for edits, commands and plain work, the desk for a long command, looking around for reads, meditating while compacting, pointing, waving or the bulb while a request waits, a jump for joy, a facepalm and dizziness for the events, and sways, turns, glances and a meditative doze for its visits. Clawd plays a look that runs on less: work, after 20 s, one cycle and then its first frame held for 3 s; a wait, in full for 8 s, then one cycle every 2.6 s, every 8 s after two minutes; a plan's bulb, after two minutes, a flicker every 8 s.
- Under the pointer a resting mark comes up and greets after 0.3 s, a hop or Clawd's wave, then follows the pointer with its eyes; so does a drawn bot that wears a badge. A working or waiting one keeps its look and only turns its eyes.
- On a dock that separates its agents, the mark whose own card is open is under the pointer for as long as the card is: it greets if it was resting, and keeps its look if its agent works. Closing the card gives the mark back to its agent. While a request's card from its agent stands beside it, a waiting mark turns to the card: it rises and keeps still with its eyes on the card. Clawd points at a card to its side, facing it; by a card above or below it, on a dock at the top or bottom edge, it stands still with the sign its wait wears, since pointing would aim past the card. A wait with no card, such as an approval Claude Code asks for only in its own dialog, plays its look.
- A Dynamic dock or Simple panel built again while its bots were out, as when it comes back after a card took its place, takes up each bot's look at once where its loop begins, with no morph from the logo and no intro.
- A reminder's mark opens as the agent's logo and turns into its bot 0.7 s later, once its card has opened, and stays its bot while the card shows. A request's bot asks for what it waits for. A reply's bot smiles, and Clawd jumps for joy, or dances for the end of a turn of ten minutes or more. A quota window that reset, or usage resets added to an account, are good news the bot greets, Clawd with a spark. A window used up shows the logo wearing `⧗`; a warning, the plain logo.
- The logo turns into a drawn bot in half a second: the outline relaxes, the artwork fades into the mark's colours and the eyes open.
- One timer steps every bot, waking only when a frame is due, on a grid every bot shares: every 1/24 s for a morph, a move, a blink or a hop, 1/12 s for eyes following the pointer and every frame of a sprite, 1/6 s for a breath, and not at all while every mark rests or holds still. A bot nobody can see, in a covered window, on sleeping displays or while another user has the screen, is left out until it can be seen, and then picks up where the clock is without playing what it missed.
- Reduce Motion, or `botLogos` off, leaves every mark its logo, and a mark that has a waiting request or a badge wears its sign over the logo, still; a mark with no news shows its logo at half strength.

### Type sizes

- Two tiers. What a dock answers a glance with is written to the dock's scale; what opens as a card has one fixed scale.
- The dock's scale makes a compact reminder's pill and the Simple panel's hover line as tall as one of the dock's marks: a Simple panel's ring (its Size, 20–60 pt, whether or not the rings are drawn) or a Dynamic dock's logo (20–60 pt). Both are 26 pt high at scale 1, so the scale is the mark's size over 26. It is never below 1: beside a mark of 26 pt or less, which both defaults are (a 22 pt ring, a 20 pt logo), everything is written as at the default. The notch has no size to follow and its scale is always 1.
- Written to the dock's scale: the Simple panel's numbers and hover line, the Dynamic dock's hover bubble, and a compact reminder, with their marks, gaps and heights.
- Every word a dock writes is one size: a reminder's name and status, the Simple panel's numbers and its hover line are 9 pt at scale 1 (`IslandRootView.dockText`), 10 pt beside a 30 pt ring, 12 pt beside a 36 pt one, 17 pt beside a 50 pt one and 21 pt beside a 60 pt one, so they read as one piece.
- A compact reminder on a dock is a slim pill, as tall as the Simple panel's hover line: 26 pt at the default for a 17 pt mark and 9 pt words, 30 pt beside a 30 pt ring for a 20 pt mark and 10 pt words. Its name and status are the dock's one text size, under those beside the notch (a 13 pt name, a 12 pt status), and the symbol beside the status is as much under the notch's as the status is, so they sit in the pill with room above and below; the agent's mark keeps its size. It is as long as what it says: who on one side and what on the other, each as wide as its words and no wider than one of the notch's wings (112 pt), 14 pt apart. Beside the notch the two are wings of that fixed width with the camera between them.
- Cards — the usage panel, an agent's own panel, a reminder's card — use one size per role: the agent's name 13 semibold, at most one 15 pt headline, rows 12, a status beside the name 12 medium, secondary text 11. 10 pt is for badges and counts only.

### What the Simple panel shows

- One item per mark, in row order, for the enabled windows that are not API-billed; windows that share artwork share an item.
- An agent billed by use, with a balance and no window, follows them in the balances' order: its mark, an empty ring, and its balance where the percentage would be, in the balance's level colour (a dash before its first reading). The hover line of such an agent reads its balance.
- A screen whose layout lists its agents ([panel layout](#panel-layout)) shows those of these items, in the list's order.
- An item reports its most used window among the readings that show a level, as the menu bar's figure does: a ring round the mark filled to that share, the whole percentage beside it, or both. Each can be switched off, and with both off the panel is the marks alone. A window with no reading shows an empty ring and a dash.
- The ring and the number take that window's level colour. No glow is drawn behind it, collapsed or open.
- Its marks follow `botLogos` and act out what their agents are doing, as the dock's do ([bots](#bots)). With `botLogos` off, or Reduce Motion on, nothing in the panel moves, and a mark wears a waiting request's or a badge's sign over its logo.
- One length sizes the panel, 20–60 pt: the ring's diameter, with the mark scaled from it and the number written at the dock's text size. The ring's stroke is 1–4 pt. Its Logo spacing, between two agents, is 0.1–0.6 of the ring, 0.45 at first; marks alone in a row, with no numbers, sit at 70 % of it.
- It parks and drags like the dock, 3 pt in from its edge: a row on the top and bottom, a column on the sides with the numbers lined up on the right. Hovering grows the panel itself into the usage panel: the same frame, still 3 pt off its edge and rounded on every corner, enlarges inward round the figures, which keep their place, and shrinks back into the small panel on leaving. An event takes the figures' place in that frame while it shows.

### Hovering and events

- A visible dock accepts dragging; with logos hidden, clicks pass through.
- Hovering opens inward, optionally requiring Option, with the same opening and closing timing on all four edges. During the delay, content height is measured and glow and shadow bitmaps are drawn off the UI thread without retaining a hidden panel; leaving cancels preparation and discards late results. Opening and collapse commit their window geometry immediately, with materials installed when ready. Matching installed materials are reused without another raster.
- Events appear once, on the pointer's display.
- A successfully ended turn shows its conversation title and, for Codex, the last paragraph of its final reply when available, with a blue outlined speech bubble. Replies and permission requests share an event panel, separate from the usage panel, with one card open and the rest selectable. A reply offers session return when an exact target is available and a separate token-usage action; a failed return retains it for retry. See [session navigation](session-navigation.md) for coverage.
- The completion's token-usage action opens its Sessions statistics page; quota events reveal and highlight their Tokens card, and other events open Tokens. A panel session's title returns to its native agent when available, its token count opens that session's statistics, and the heading opens the list; an agent's logo and name above its quota windows return to its newest session that has a destination; chart and menu-bar controls open Tokens and reset Sessions to its list.
- Opening statistics or settings collapses the HUD until the pointer leaves and returns; controls that change only the panel keep it open.
- A dock never hides its marks. An event, a reply's pill or a request's card, comes out of the mark of the agent that sent it, beside the dock on its inner side, and every other mark stays where it is; an agent with no mark on that dock has its card come out of the dock's middle. A Simple panel stays as it is at rest beside the card. News that arrives while the whole usage panel is open is one of its rows. Notch mode has no marks: it sits at a fixed place over hardware with no pixels to draw on, and an event grows wings out of the island.
- A dock's backdrop stops when the panel opens, and goes on beside an event's card; only notch mode draws a rim.
- The expanded panel flares outward at both ends of its contact with the parked edge, while its text stays upright; the marks keep their saved position over the open usage panel, even when its content is shorter than the queue.

### Usage panel

- In its default layout the panel shows quota and balances, then Tokens, then the local session summary with at most five rows in total: sessions with work in flight, those waiting for the user ahead of those still running and newest created first within each group, then sessions last active in the past 24 hours by their last event. Account-wide API receipts and older ended sessions remain in statistics and do not occupy these rows or enter their remaining count. Each row shows its agent's mark and its state on the mark's corner. The remaining count covers every hidden session in this summary; it and the header open the full Sessions page. A layout can give the summary 1 to 10 rows, reach back seven days in place of 24 hours, list every session by its last event, the newest first, whatever its state, in place of those in flight first, and list only some agents', some projects' or some states' sessions ([panel layout](#panel-layout)): the rows keep the layout's order, sessions with work in flight are listed whatever the period, the header counts the running sessions the filter keeps, whether or not they are among the rows, the remaining count covers what it keeps beyond the rows, and a filter that keeps none of the summary's sessions says No sessions match in one line under the header, which then gives no count or state, where a period without sessions says No sessions yet under Recently ended.
- A session waiting on a question shows a badge on its row. It opens the question right there, answered the same way the island's own card answers it, beside a way back to the agent for answering it in its own dialog instead. A row's title returns to its agent, or opens that session's page where the client names no destination; its token count opens that session's usage.
- The token chart is a compact strip with the latest 24 hours in 96 quarter-hour bars, including the current quarter, and all token kinds. Three small buttons in its header, `24h`, `7d` and `30d`, flip it to the last 7 or 30 days, rolling and ending with the same quarter, in the local-day bars the statistics window uses for such a span, 7 or 8 and 30 or 31 of them; the total beside them says the period (`7d · 332M tok`). The picker starts from the layout's period, `24h` by default, each time the panel opens, and a flip lasts until the panel closes and is never saved. The bars are 40 pt tall in every period, and the picker takes no more height than the header's text, so a flip never resizes the panel; a flip fades the bars over, at once with Reduce Motion. A quota block's chart widget keeps a period of its own, the latest 24 hours unless its layout gives it another, whatever the token chart shows. Each period's columns are built once for each report and kept, as the panel asks for them on every pass. A layout can narrow the chart to some agents' models and to some models by id; Dashboard range, bucket, model and token-kind controls do not change it, and it has no token-kind filter; its legend, axis and peak mark belong to the statistics window. Height follows content up to the display's height minus 80 pt; overflow scrolls above fixed settings, statistics and host controls, clear of the notch and dock.

### Panel layout

- The panel has three sections: the agents' blocks, the token chart and the sessions, in that order by default. A block is one agent's quota windows, under its vendor id (`Claude`), or the balances of the accounts one API provider bills, under `api:` and the provider (`api:DeepSeek`), each account's card under its own divider. A quota block's rows are its windows, each under its provider's own window name (`claude-weekly`), which stands for that window on every account that has it.
- A layout records how a panel differs from the default view and nothing else: for a block, the widget that draws it, the measures its picker offers and in what order, whether it shows its reset countdown, how many cells stand on a line and how large its rings are, whether it takes the whole line or half of one, the period a chart draws or a balance's estimated cost, burn rate and chart cover, whether it is hidden and which of its rows are, the order of its rows and which of its accounts it shows; the order of the blocks; the order of the sections; the sections that are hidden; whether the quota blocks show their reset countdowns; and the panel's style, its width, its spacing and whether lines divide its blocks and sections; how many session rows the panel shows, how far back they reach, in what order, and which agents, projects and states they list; the token chart's period and whose tokens it draws, by agent and by model; and the agents its screen's resting HUD shows. Blocks and sections it does not name follow in default order, and a block with all of its rows hidden is left out. A key or an id that names no block, row or account the panel has now is passed over and stays in the layout, which applies to it again when it returns. A layout that comes through the tools is kept as what it changes: a widget that is the block's default, measures that are every one its widget offers in the widget's own order, four columns, a medium ring, the whole line, what a block has nothing to set for, a chart's `day` period, a balance's period where it is the token chart's, a block's place in the order where it would come there anyway, and a row's place in its block's order where it would come there anyway, are left out, and so are five session rows, a session period of `day`, the `active` order, every state listed and the token chart's `day` period, so the default view spelled out in full is still the default layout. A block's accounts and its own countdown are kept as written, as what a layout holds for an account that is away is: accounts that name every account a block has now still leave out an account that comes back or joins later, and a block's countdown that is the layout's now still holds once Settings' Reset countdown is turned the other way.
- The four switches of Settings → Display → Expanded panel are fields of every layout, not settings of their own. Session list, Quota details and Token usage are whether the layout shows its `sessions`, `blocks` and `tokens` sections, which `hiddenSections` lists, Quota details covering every quota block and every balance, and Reset countdown is the layout's `countdown`, which a block's own `countdown` overrides either way. The switches show and set the layout that the display chosen in Settings → Display → Screens keeps saved: its own where it keeps one, else the shared one, which every display without a layout of its own draws. A person's switch sets that layout at once, and a waiting draft of it with it, the shared draft or the display's own, so the panel shows the change either way; it sets that one field, and like a person's own edit of a display it is no version, so Revert to Previous still goes back to the version saved before. The section says which layout the switches set: the chosen display's own, which no other display draws, or the shared one, with the attached displays that keep a layout of their own, which it leaves, and that choosing one of them above sets its switches. Like every other field of a layout, they are an agent's to draft, the person's to save, a screen's own layout's to set apart, and Revert to Previous and Reset to Default bring them back with the rest; the default view has all four on. Every window and account keeps its switch and its place in Settings → Agents, which no layout brings back.
- Earlier versions kept the four switches in the settings. At the first launch after one of them, each switch it kept off is carried into every saved layout, the shared one and each screen's own and the layout saved before each, so that every panel and every Revert to Previous draws what it drew; the settings are then written again without them, so they are carried once. A switch read as something other than true or false counts as on, as it was by default, and settings that cannot be read whole are not written over.
- A block's `rows` orders its rows, its windows, by key: the rows it names come first, in that order, and the others keep their order after them, in each of its accounts. Its `accounts` chooses which of its accounts it shows, by the id `get_hud` gives each, every one where it names none: a quota block's accounts are the sections its rows are drawn in, each under its account's header, and an API balance block's are its accounts' cards. Rows that belong to no account, which no id names, are left out by any choice. A block whose accounts keep none of the accounts it has now, or whose rows of those it keeps are all hidden, is left out, as a block with every row hidden is. A row or an account named that the block does not have now stays in the layout and applies again when it returns.
- A Mac keeps a shared layout and, for any screen, a layout of its own. A screen with its own layout draws it whole, with nothing of the shared one mixed in, not even what its own leaves at the default; a screen without one draws the shared layout. An agent's own panel on a dock that separates its agents follows its screen's layout. A screen that is not attached keeps its own layout for when it returns. Mode, edge and the rest of a screen's placement and glow stay in Settings → Display, per screen as before, and no layout sets them: an agent's change to them is a [style draft](#style-drafts).
- A layout also lists the agents its screen's resting HUD shows (`hud.agents`), each matched by the mark it is drawn with, as the HUD merges them: `Codex` names the mark Codex and ChatGPT share. The list narrows and orders what the screen would show anyway: the Dynamic dock's marks, the Simple panel's items, and the glow's parts, so its colours, the place an alert's pulse lights and its pace, the working period only while one of those agents has a session in flight. It never adds an agent, an agent listed that the screen does not show now is passed over, and a list that names none of the agents it shows now leaves the screen showing every agent rather than an empty HUD. Without a list every agent is shown as it comes. On a dock that separates its agents the marks follow the list, and so do the card a mark opens and the mark an event's card grows from; an event from an agent the screen does not show opens as the whole HUD. A change of list, a draft included, redraws that screen's closed HUD at once, with the HUD's own motion.
- The menu bar follows the same list, by the same rule. The menu-bar menu lists the vendors and the balances the HUD of the screen whose menu bar it opens from shows, a balance by its vendor as the Simple panel takes it, in the list's order, the vendors' quota rows before the balances as before. The status item has one figure and one set of colours for every menu bar, so they follow the HUD of the main display, the first display the system lists, whose menu bar is the only one with Displays have separate Spaces off: the figure is the most used window among the agents that HUD shows whose readings show a level, and the colours are the levels of its glow's parts, in their order. The list narrows the menu bar as it narrows the HUD, in the mode the display draws now, a style draft included, so a list that names none of the agents that HUD shows now leaves the menu bar showing every agent too, and without a list, or without a display, it shows every agent as it always has. A block or a window the layout hides from the panel stays in the menu bar, as it stays on the resting HUD. Both follow a draft as the HUD does: the figure and its colours change at once, as with a new reading, and the menu reads the list as it opens and keeps its rows while it is open, as it keeps them through a refresh.
- A layout's `sessions` sets the session rows: `limit`, 1 to 10 rows, 5 when absent; `period`, how long after its last event an ended session is still listed, `day`, the last 24 hours, when absent, or `week`, the last seven days, the seven days sessions are reported for and as far back as the rows look, while a session with work in flight is listed whatever the period, and the counts `get_hud` gives by state are the period's; `sort`, `active` when absent, the sessions with work in flight first, those waiting for the user ahead of those running and the newest started first within each, then the others by their last event, the newest first, or `date`, every session by its last event, the newest first, whatever its state, with the header still counting every running session the filter keeps and the remaining count what it keeps beyond the rows; `agents`, the execution agents whose sessions are listed, each matched by the mark it is drawn with, as an agent's own panel matches its sessions, so `Codex` names Codex and ChatGPT and Grok CLI and Grok Bot stay apart; `projects`, the projects whose sessions are listed, as the statistics window's Sessions page groups them: a Git repository's common root, so that its worktrees are one project, else the folder the session ran in, each by its id, the folder written with `~`, or `unassigned` for the sessions of no folder, the page's Unassigned project; and `states`, from `waiting`, `running` and `ended`, in which a session whose Live status is off or whose reading is out of date counts as ended, as its row shows it. Absent or empty agents, projects or states list them all. `get_hud` lists the projects the sessions Agent HUD holds belong to in `sessions.offeredProjects`, each with its `id` and its `name`, the folder's last part or Unassigned project, in the page's order, the sessions of no folder last; an agent can also name a project by a path, absolute or written with `~`, that lies inside it as written, through a link, or as Git places a worktree, the innermost project first, so that its own working directory names the project it works in, and the layout keeps the project's id. A session's project is found once for each folder the report names, from Git's own pointers, and kept for that report, so the panel's passes read nothing more for it. Its `tokens` sets the chart: `period`, `day`, `week` or `month`, the period the picker starts from; `agents`, whose models' tokens the chart draws, each model matched by its vendor as the panel's blocks are keyed (`AgentDescriptor.displayVendor`), as a quota block's chart matches its models, and by mark; and `models`, the models it draws by the id `get_hud` gives each in `tokens.offeredModels`, among the agents' models where `agents` names any, so a model of an agent `agents` leaves out is refused, as is an id the chart has no model of now that the layout does not already hold. Absent or empty agents or models chart them all. An agent's own panel on a dock that separates its agents applies its agent and these filters together: its rows are its agent's sessions the filter keeps, and its chart draws its agent's models among those the layout names, none where the layout names other agents or only other agents' models. The chart takes its models from each report, so a model that starts spending tokens while the panel is open is drawn with the next report. An agent, a project or a model named here that has no session or no tokens now matches nothing until it has, and stays in the layout.
- Only the hover panel, its screen's resting HUD and, as above, the menu bar follow a layout. Quota alerts, permission requests and replies, iPhone sync, statistics and other screens show every agent as they did; the Sessions page that the remaining count opens lists every session, whatever the layout's filter.

- Rings and numbers stand four to a line, or two to six as the layout sets. A last line with fewer windows keeps the columns' width and stands centred under the lines above, so three windows two to a line make a triangle; a single line shares the width among its windows. A ring is `medium` by default, 46 pt with a 4 pt line and an 11 pt figure; `small` is 40 pt with a 3 pt line and a 10 pt figure, and `large` 60 pt with a 5 pt line and a 13 pt figure. The figure fits inside the ring at 100% in every size, and in a cell narrower than its ring the ring is as wide as the cell. A window's name too long for its cell gives way to its short name, and a short name too long for one line, as a Chinese one can be in six columns of an agent's own panel, wraps. A number too wide for its cell is written shorter: a burn rate in whole points (`12%/h`), a cost in thousands or millions as the language writes them (`$12K`, `$1.2万`). The line under the name gives way to its short form, and where that does not fit either the line is left empty and keeps its height.
- Account headers, wallet amounts and Codex's earned resets stay under every widget. A balance's measures read the balance its provider reported and the costs Agent HUD recorded for its account's requests, priced as the Tokens page prices them, and start no read. `quota` is the balance beside the estimated cost, the card as it has always been; `burn` is the burn rate, the estimated cost per day over the period so far, the days without a recorded request included (`¥0.10/day`), beside when the balance runs out at that rate from the time it was read, written as a quota window's run-out is within a week, a weekday and a time, then a date and a time, and from a year on as the month and the year, with Exhausted for a balance at or below zero, No usage where nothing was spent over the period, and a dash where the balance or the burn rate is not known or the balance's reading shows no level; and `cost` is the estimated cost beside the burn rate. A `period` on a quota block not drawn as a chart is refused, as it covers none.

Where separators are on, a thin line stands down the middle of the gap, and the shared line has one divider above it where a quota block would have one, a balance in it included. A half block with no half block left next to it, as the third of three in a row, takes the whole line, so a line never has an empty half. Bar rows keep the whole line, since their columns take about 366 pt; a stored half on bar rows is the whole line, and a proposal of one is refused. Two halves share a line only where each is at least 230 pt wide: room for the heading of the longest name the app gives an agent, GitHub Copilot's, with its logo and a picker of four measures, and for two cells as wide as the four the narrowest whole panel, 480 pt, gives its windows by default, 102 pt each. ### Panel holds

- While an agent edits a layout through the MCP tools ([command line](command-line.md#mcp-server)), the panel it edits stays open on screen whether or not the pointer is over it, so the person sees each change as it is made. A panel the hold keeps open, the one a layout edit for the same `screen` would hold, closes so the HUD shows, unless the pointer is on it, and the next layout edit opens it again; the two minutes run on meanwhile, and such a call renews nothing. A `hud` style beside an `app` and nothing else is such a call too.

### Approvals

Codex Desktop's Computer Use app-access prompts and its browser's prompts come from the Desktop client that owns the thread, over local IPC. The HUD follows each thread with a session in flight: the owner sends the thread's state once and then what changes, so a prompt shows as it is raised and goes as it is answered, in a conversation of any length. An app prompt offers **Deny**, **Allow this conversation**, and **Always allow** only when Desktop advertises that scope. A browser prompt, such as the one for a website the agent opens for the first time, offers **Deny**, **Allow once** or, where the request offers it, **Allow this conversation**, and **Always allow** with the site it adds written out; Desktop's own **Allow for all sites** stays in Desktop. A browser prompt shows only in a turn the user reviews, as with the hook. Before answering, the HUD confirms the same owner and exact request are still waiting. Answering in Desktop withdraws the card; leaving, expiry, and a thread whose state is not known never answer it. Client hooks off stops observation and cancels answers still in preflight. Engine tool approvals continue through the permission hook and its reviewer check.

Codex Desktop's mid-turn **Question** cards also appear on the HUD, with the supplied choices and a reply field, including questions without choices. Their questions are read from the active turn's messages in the same followed state. Answers go back to the same active turn after the owner and question are checked again. **Skip** hides the HUD card without sending a reply. An accepted answer in Desktop or the end of the turn withdraws the card.

The Claude desktop app's own questions reach the HUD from the session log, since they go through no permission prompt and no hook reports them: the next-step box a turn ends with (`mcp__ccd_turn__wrap_up` with an `ask`), and the card offering a task its own session (`mcp__ccd_session__start_session` with `initiation` `offer`). Only these two calls have their input read. The card shows the question — for an offer, the task's title — the box's recap, and the buttons the app shows, with the words each sends on hover; an app action's button carries the app's own words. Agent HUD Open answers neither: the app takes the click in its own window, and the card says to answer in Claude; Dismiss only hides it. A host that can send words into the session as the user's next message, such as the private app with its Claude plugin, makes a box's card a question card instead (`clientReply`): an answer sends what a click on the box sends, the chosen button's message, else its label, or the user's own words, and a box left unanswered sends nothing. An offer is only shown either way, since its answer cannot be put in words. The session waits as a question until the log says it was answered — an offer by its call's result, the box by the next prompt, whoever sent it — or for 30 minutes after it was asked, after which the HUD stops vouching that anyone is waiting. The words are not stored: after a relaunch they are read again from the call's own line, whose place in the log is all the ledger keeps.

Antigravity supplies waiting permissions through its running local service, without an execution hook. All services advertising local credentials are read; agy without them is excluded. Before sending allow-once or deny, the HUD rereads the exact interaction. Answers in Antigravity remove the card. Leaving or expiry keeps the native prompt unanswered and the card hidden until a complete reading confirms it ended. Client hooks off stops observation and cancels answers still in preflight.

DeepSeek Harness questions can be answered on the HUD when its web host exposes the compatible question interface. The HUD confirms the host before reading its loopback event stream; hosts using a different interface keep questions in Harness. No hook is installed and nothing is written to Harness's files. Pending questions replay when the connection returns, answers keep each question's original id, and a question settled in Harness leaves the HUD. Plan reviews and questions with supporting detail stay in Harness so their full content can be read. Leaving or expiry keeps the question open there and hidden on the HUD, including across a temporary connection loss. Client hooks off stops the reading. See [the supported interface](providers.md#deepseek-harness).

- A hook client that stops to ask whether a tool may run reaches the HUD through a socket of its own, and the request lives only as long as that client waits for it. One copy of the application runs at a time ([command line](command-line.md#launch-and-display)) and serves the socket; should another start beside it with the same data directory, it leaves the socket to the first, and quitting removes only a socket that copy made. Answering resumes the client; the client giving up — timed out, killed — takes the request off the HUD by itself, and nothing is answered on anyone's behalf.
- While a request waits, its session needs approval wherever it is shown — its dot among the island's sessions, its card on the Sessions page and its page — and counts as running, whatever the client's log says; the mark leaves with the request. A session whose Live status is off shows no state.
- Claude Code keeps its hook waiting after the user answers in Claude Code's own dialog, in the terminal or the desktop app. The hook follows the session record instead: once the call it asked about has its result there — answered, refused or interrupted — it leaves without an answer, which takes the request off the HUD. The record names the call by its tool and exact input; an approved command's result is written when the command finishes, so its card stays until then or until the wait runs out.
- A request holds the island until it is settled, where other events expire a few seconds after being shown. Unanswered, it waits Settings → General → Wait for an answer — 10 minutes unless 1, 3, 5, 30 or 60 is chosen — and then goes back to the client's own prompt, answered by nobody. The hook timeout written into the client, a day, is only the ceiling behind that wait, so a new value applies at once, to requests already waiting too, without rewriting any client's settings. Quitting the application hands every waiting request back the same way, and a client that asks while it is not running goes straight to its own prompt. Replies that arrive while a request waits remain selectable in the event panel; quota events and added usage resets are dropped, and a second request waits its turn.
- Hovering opens the queue: the oldest request open, the rest a line each. Any line can be opened, which closes the one before it, and the answers always act on the open one. Answering hands over to whichever has waited longest.
- A request is on one display at a time, the one the pointer was on when it arrived, and the queue lists every request waiting on any of them: opening a line that arrived on another display moves that request to this one. A request whose display goes away moves to the display the pointer is on, still waiting.
- A question Claude Code, ZCode or DeepSeek Harness asks its user is answered rather than approved: each question with its offered answers, several where it allows them, and a field for the user's own words, one question at a time and sent together after the last. Any question can be skipped; the client hears which were left open — nothing on a question card refuses the call. A session waiting on one carries a badge on its row in the usage panel, where the same answers open inline.
- A plan Claude Code asks to have approved is answered on the card, with the plan itself above the answers in a box that scrolls, so nothing is approved unread: Approve, after which Claude Code still asks about each edit; Approve, accept edits, after which the session accepts edits without asking; and Keep planning, a plain outlined button that opens a field for what should change, which may be left empty, and Send. The approval goes back with the plan as the call's input, which Claude Code needs to take it, and accepting edits as the mode the session moves to; keeping on planning tells Claude the user reviewed the plan in Agent HUD and did not approve it, with their words if they wrote any. Claude Code's own plan dialog is there meanwhile, and whichever answer comes first counts. A plan from any other client is not answered on the HUD: the card names it and points to the client, and putting it away answers nothing. The field is the only thing on the HUD that takes the keyboard, and only when clicked — a card that arrives never catches keys typed elsewhere; the keyboard goes back to the app in front when the answer is sent, Escape is pressed or another window is clicked, and the island stays open while it is being typed into.
- Deny and allow-once are always offered for every other request, and always beside what an allow lets run: the command, the file and the lines it would change, the URL or the tool's own input, even when the one-line summary already says the same. A request that arrives while the usage panel is open switches to the event panel.
- A third answer appears only when the client supports rule updates and itself suggested a rule — the HUD echoes that suggestion back untouched rather than composing one, and writes out the rule it adds, such as `Bash(npm test:*)`, before it can be given. Antigravity, Codex, CodeBuddy, WorkBuddy, ZCode and Qwen Code offer only deny and allow-once; their shell commands, file edits and MCP tools use the same queue as Claude Code.
- A request reaches the HUD only for a user's decision; automatic review and requests whose reviewer cannot be confirmed continue in the client's own permission flow without a HUD answer. A client whose hook runs before every tool call, or whose hook cannot approve, is not connected, because answering it would mean asking about calls the client would have allowed on its own. A plan approval from any client but Claude Code, and a question from any client but Claude Code, ZCode, DeepSeek Harness and Codex Desktop's mid-turn Question interface, stays in the client's own dialog: those clients either ignore an answer sent back, act on one without the user's reply, or are not known to read one.
- A Claude Code run with no one else to ask never reaches the HUD: `claude -p`, or an SDK host, started without `--permission-prompt-tool` or with `--permission-prompts none`, in a session Claude Code itself marks unattended (`CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ATTENDED=0` on the hook). Claude Code denies such a request as soon as its hooks return, so the hook returns at once with no answer and the run goes on as it would with no HUD installed. A run started from another session's shell inherits that session's entrypoint, so the entrypoint is not read for this. Terminal sessions, the desktop app and IDE hosts, SDK hosts that ask their own user (they pass a prompt tool and hear the request while the hook waits) and background sessions are shown as before, and so is a run the hook cannot place.
- Settings → General → Client hooks switches every handler Agent HUD keeps in the clients' own settings: approvals, Claude Code's notification hook, the stop hooks, Pi's extension and OpenCode's plugin. Switching it off asks first, naming what stops working — answering requests on the HUD, completion reminders from the clients that report them only through a stop hook or a plugin, Claude Code's waiting state and Pi's running status; usage, quota and sessions are unaffected. Off, every Agent HUD handler is removed at once, whichever copy of the application added it, and none is added back; ZCode's hooks switch stays as it was.
- Saying nothing is an answer the HUD can always give, and it is what a closed, paused or busy HUD gives: the client's own permission flow carries on as though no hook were installed. A hidden or paused glow silences events but never a request, which would otherwise leave a session waiting with nothing on screen to say why.

### The glow

- A display in Simple mode draws no glow, and its settings show no glow section. Every other display has its own glow: style, effect, speed, reach and density are set per screen, and a display with none of its own follows the default.
- A display whose layout lists its agents ([panel layout](#panel-layout)) draws only their parts of the glow, in the list's order, an alert's pulse lights its sender's part among them, or the whole glow for a sender it does not show, and its effect plays at the working period only while one of them has a session in flight. The glow preview in Settings follows the same list.
- The falloff is two settings, both counted in rows: how many keep full strength, and how many the glow fades away over. The fade is Gaussian, so it leaves the solid rows level instead of dropping at once.
- The effect plays at the working period while any agent runs and at the idle period otherwise; the glow never stops, it only slows down, so a resting HUD still reads as alive.
- Every effect takes the period, not only breathing. Grid styles are dithered by each cell's place in the Bayer matrix, which keeps the average density and breaks up the stripes a distance-only level would produce.
- The whole HUD shares one drawing budget across displays, so a second screen costs frames rather than processor. A blurred glow never enters the frame loop; an idle one is sampled at 8 frames a second.
- Reduce Motion holds the resting frame and turns the island's geometry changes into a cross-fade.

`get_hud` reads the app's `appearance`, `appearanceFollowsScreen`, `botLogos`, `showMenuBarIcon`, `language`, `requiresOptionToOpen`, `openImmediatelyAtTop`, `hoverDelayMs` and `collapseDelayMs` under `app`, as the app draws them with any app draft, the first five as `appearance`, `matchScreen`, `botLogos`, `showMenuBarIcon` and `language`, with `writtenIn`, the language the panel's names are written in now, and the last four under `hover` as `pressOptionToOpen`, `openAtTopEdge`, `delayMs` and `collapseDelayMs`; `draft_hud` takes them, but for the language, as an [app draft](#app-drafts), which only a person's Save writes ([command line](command-line.md#mcp-server)). `SettingsStore.effectiveSettings` is the settings with the app draft drawn over them, which every place that draws these settings reads, and `updateSetting(_:_:)` a person's edit of one, which drops its field from the draft first.

Supported approval hooks are installed for detected clients at startup, alongside the notification and completion hooks, unless `clientHooks` is off; `--permission-hook <source>` is the handler they point back at. Antigravity uses its running service instead, and DeepSeek Harness's questions its web host. See [command line](command-line.md) and [data access](data-access.md).

`Settings.placement(on:hasNotch:)` and `glow(on:)` answer what one display uses, falling back to the default when it has none of its own. Both are keyed by the string `ScreenIdentity.key(for:)` returns for a display. `SettingsStore.effectiveLook(on:)` answers the look one display's HUD draws now ([look](#look)).

The panel's layouts are stored apart from these settings, under `panelLayout.v2` in the same preferences, as one value: `{"shared": {"current": …, "previous": …}, "screens": {"<display UUID>": {"current": …, "previous": …, "previousStyle": …}}}`. `current` is the saved layout and `previous` the one saved before it; a screen's side is a layout or the string `"shared"`, and a screen's `current` left out is the shared layout. A screen's `previousStyle` is its own placement and glow in the version before, `{"placement": …, "glow": …}` as `screens` and `screenGlow` keep them, each left out where it had none of its own; it is left out itself where that version had the style the screen has now. The shared side is left out while it is the default layout with no previous one, a screen while it uses the shared layout with nothing before it, and a draft is never stored. The first format, `panelLayout.v1`, was never released and is not read. A layout is a JSON object, and `{}` is the default view:

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| `blocks.<block key>.widget` / `.measures` | `bar`, `ring`, `number`, `chart` / the measures the picker offers, in order, from `quota`, `burn`, `tokens`, `cost`; absent or empty for every measure the widget offers; an API balance takes `number`, with `quota`, `burn` and `cost`, or `chart`, with `cost` |
| `blocks.<block key>.hidden` / `.hiddenRows` | `true` leaves the block out / the keys of the rows left out of it |

| `blocks.<block key>.accounts` | The ids of the accounts the block shows (`ProviderAccount.id` for a quota account, `APIBilling.id` for an API account, as `get_hud` lists them), each once, in id order, kept as written, so that a list naming every account the block has now still leaves out one that joins later; absent or empty for every account |
| `blocks.<block key>.countdown` | `true` or `false`: whether a quota block shows its reset times, whatever `countdown` says, kept as written where it is what `countdown` says now; absent follows `countdown` |

| `sections` / `hiddenSections` | `blocks`, `tokens` and `sessions` in the order they come / those left out, which Settings' Quota details, Token usage and Session list switch in the saved layout of the display chosen in Settings → Display |

A stored layout is read forgivingly, field by field: a widget, ring size, span, spacing, sort or period name this build does not know counts as not given, a session period the build before named reads as the period that covers most of it, `3d` and `7d` as `week` and `1h`, `2h`, `6h`, `12h` and `24h` as `day`, a measure, section or state it does not know is left out of its list, columns outside 2–6, a width outside 480–720 and a session limit outside 1–10 count as not given, a `countdown` that is not true or false counts as not given, a row key or an account id that is not a string is left out of its list, a row or an account the block does not have now is passed over, and a widget, measure, countdown, columns, ring size, half a line or period a block does not take falls back to the block's default. Each side and each screen is read on its own, so one that cannot be read leaves the rest: an unreadable shared `current` is the default layout, an unreadable screen `current` the shared layout, an unreadable `previous` or `previousStyle` none, and a screen that is not an object is left out. `SettingsStore.panelLayout` is the store (`PanelLayoutStore`); a screen's panel and resting HUD draw its `effective(for:)` layout, keyed by the string `ScreenIdentity.key(for:)` returns. Its `switches(on:)` (`PanelSwitches`: `sessionList`, `quotaDetails`, `resetCountdown`, `tokenUsage`) reads the four Expanded panel switches from the layout a display keeps saved, its own or the shared one, `switches` from the shared one, and `setSwitches(on:_:)` sets them, as Settings does for the display chosen there.

Settings written by an earlier version keep the four switches under `showIslandSessions`, `showIslandQuota`, `showResetCountdown` and `showIslandTokens` in `settings.v1`, which this version no longer reads as settings or writes. At launch, where any of them is there, `SettingsStore` carries each that is `false` into every saved layout, the shared `current` and `previous` and each screen's own, and writes the settings again without them where it read them whole.
